Project 04 · Quantum photonics

Wafer-level optics for cold-atom systems

Planar optics combining beam steering and polarization control for compact beam delivery.

Quantum opticsPolarization controlInverse design

The application

Magneto-optical traps use beams with controlled directions and circular polarization to cool and capture atoms. Conventional beam-delivery arrangements rely on bulk optics and careful alignment.

The research approach

Our work proposes a two-wafer optical architecture using an inverse-designed grating coupler and metasurface retroreflectors. The devices combine functions otherwise performed by separate waveplates and mirrors, targeting rubidium-87 operation at 780 nm.

My contribution

I contributed to inverse design, electromagnetic modeling, and the fabrication and optical characterization of multifunctional photonic devices. This work formed part of my research at NIST.

Results and evidence

Simulated out-coupling efficiency, with polarization ellipticity of 0.85.

The two-wafer system is a proposed beam-delivery architecture. The reported retroreflector was experimentally characterized; the grating-coupler results are full-wave simulations. This work does not demonstrate a complete operating atom trap.

Research outcome

Published in ACS Photonics. The project connects inverse-designed optics to the beam-delivery requirements of compact cold-atom systems.
